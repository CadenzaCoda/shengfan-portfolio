    <section id="idea" class="section wrap intro-grid" aria-labelledby="idea-title">
      <div><p class="eyebrow">The idea</p><h2 id="idea-title">Imitation accuracy<br>is only part of the story.</h2></div>
      <div class="prose"><p>At the handling limits, a small deviation from an expert’s action can send a vehicle off track. Expert demonstrations can also be unreliable or partially unsafe. Minimizing imitation error alone may leave a policy brittle.</p><p><strong>CAIL adds a learned safety penalty to behavior cloning.</strong> Motivated by a differentiable approximation of a predictive safety filter, it trains the policy to account for constraint satisfaction alongside imitation accuracy.</p><p>This is safety-filter-guided imitation through a joint training objective. The deployed policy does not run a separate safety filter.</p></div>
    </section>

    <section id="method" class="section wrap" aria-labelledby="method-title">
      <div class="section-heading"><div><p class="eyebrow">The method</p><h2 id="method-title">Two training signals.<br>One deployed policy.</h2></div><p>Full-state information supplies privileged safety supervision during training, including when the policy learns from images and velocity measurements.</p></div>
      <div class="mode-switch" role="group" aria-label="Highlight a stage of the method"><button type="button" class="active" data-stage="training" aria-pressed="true">During training</button><button type="button" data-stage="deployment" aria-pressed="false">At deployment</button></div>
      <div class="method-board" data-mode="training">
        <div class="method-flow"><div class="flow-block"><span class="step-label">Observation</span><strong>State or<br>images + velocity</strong></div><span class="flow-arrow" aria-hidden="true">→</span><div class="flow-block policy"><span class="step-label">Learned policy</span><strong>π<sub>θ</sub></strong></div><span class="flow-arrow" aria-hidden="true">→</span><div class="flow-block"><span class="step-label">Action</span><strong>Throttle<br>+ steering</strong></div></div>
        <div class="supervision"><div><span class="step-label">Performance supervision</span><h3>Imitate the expert</h3><p>Match expert actions with a mean-squared imitation loss.</p></div><span class="plus" aria-hidden="true">+</span><div><span class="step-label">Safety supervision</span><h3>Account for what happens next</h3><p>Use learned dynamics and safe-set membership to penalize predicted unsafe behavior.</p></div></div>
        <p id="stage-note" class="stage-note" aria-live="polite">Training: the expert and learned safety models provide supervision. Rollouts and expert labels are collected iteratively.</p>
      </div>
      <p class="diagram-caption">Conceptual overview of the architecture. At deployment, the expert, learned dynamics, and safety model are not needed to generate policy actions.</p>
      <div class="method-details"><div><p class="eyebrow">The learning objective</p><p class="equation" aria-label="Total loss equals imitation loss plus lambda times the unweighted safety penalty">L<sub>CA</sub> = L<sub>clone</sub> + λ · ℓ<sub>safety</sub></p><p>The safety term is a negative log-likelihood penalty. The weight λ controls its contribution relative to imitation. Here ℓ<sub>safety</sub> denotes the unweighted penalty; Equation 14 in the paper includes λ in L<sub>safety</sub>.</p></div><div><p class="eyebrow">Learning from outcomes</p><h3>A failure is not an unsafe label for every state.</h3><p>Safety auto-labeling uses successful and failed trajectories. Local convex-hull comparisons remove likely safe states from candidate negative examples, reducing false negatives in the surrogate labels.</p><a class="text-link" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07737v2#S4">Read Section IV and Algorithm 1 <span aria-hidden="true">↗</span></a></div></div>
      <details class="technical-detail"><summary>See the paper’s gradient-computation diagram <span aria-hidden="true">+</span></summary><figure><img src="assets/training-architecture.png" width="1524" height="714" alt="Figure 2: policy learning combines imitation MSE and safety NLL; dynamics and safety classification have their own training losses." loading="lazy"><figcaption>Figure 2 from the paper. Only the policy network is used at test time; the other modules participate in training.</figcaption></figure></details>

    <section id="results" class="results-section" aria-labelledby="results-title"><div class="wrap section">
      <div class="section-heading"><div><p class="eyebrow">The evidence</p><h2 id="results-title">More consistent racing.<br>With limited observations.</h2></div><p>Three simulation experiments compare CAIL against behavior cloning with DAgger, using the same policy architecture within each experiment.</p></div>
      <div class="metrics"><div><strong>50</strong><span>consecutive laps without constraint violation</span></div><div><strong>5.65<span> s</span></strong><span>average lap time at the selected checkpoint</span></div><div><strong>81</strong><span>epoch where early stopping is triggered</span></div></div>
      <p class="result-context">Image-feedback racing, Experiment V-C / Figure 7. These values describe the reported rollout and selected checkpoint, not an average across random seeds. The expert’s average lap time is 4.805 s.</p>
      <figure class="result-figure"><a href="assets/image-racing-results.png" aria-label="Open the original image-feedback racing result figure at full size"><img src="assets/image-racing-results.png" width="1233" height="562" alt="Figure 7: baseline stays below 10 completed laps, while CAIL reaches 50 near epoch 80. Performance varies after that checkpoint. The right panel shows the selected policy's trajectory." loading="lazy"></a><figcaption><span>Figure 7 / Original paper</span>CAIL recovers a policy completing 50 laps within 80 epochs; the baseline does not exceed 10 in the reported experiment. Early stopping occurs at epoch 81.</figcaption></figure>
      <div class="experiment-list"><article><span class="experiment-number">V-A</span><div><h3>Image-feedback path following</h3><p>A PID expert tracks the center line at a conservative speed. Both methods reach 50 laps; CAIL does so in fewer training epochs.</p></div><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07737v2#S5.SS1" aria-label="Read Experiment V-A">↗</a></article><article><span class="experiment-number">V-B</span><div><h3>Full-state racing</h3><p>An MPCC expert supplies high-speed demonstrations. CAIL recovers a consistently completing policy in fewer epochs, despite slower convergence of imitation loss.</p></div><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07737v2#S5.SS2" aria-label="Read Experiment V-B">↗</a></article><article><span class="experiment-number">V-C</span><div><h3>Image-feedback racing</h3><p>The policy receives a front-facing RGB image and velocity measurements. CAIL reaches 50 consecutive laps; training variability makes checkpoint selection important.</p></div><a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07737v2#S5.SS3" aria-label="Read Experiment V-C">↗</a></article></div>
      <aside class="scope-note"><h3>What these results establish</h3><p>Empirical improvements in constraint satisfaction in simulation. The learned policy has no formal safety certificate, and physical deployment is not validated in this paper. Evaluation is capped at 50 laps; early stopping is applied when that threshold is reached for the second time.</p></aside>
