<section class="section wrap intro-grid" aria-labelledby="idea-title"><div><p class="eyebrow">The question</p><h2 id="idea-title">What if the learning signal <br>points somewhere unsafe?</h2></div><div class="prose"><p>A task objective can reward behavior that violates constraints. When demonstrations are harmful, better imitation can mean a worse controller.</p><p><strong>SCPO constrains the update itself.</strong> It evaluates the safety of candidate weight changes, builds a conservative local feasible region from those evaluations, and projects the raw gradient step into it.</p><p>Only the task loss needs gradients. Safety constraints are evaluated pointwise, so no constraint Jacobian is required.</p></div></section>

<div class="wrap"><details class="geometry-math"><summary>What is computed in this example? <span aria-hidden="true">+</span></summary><div>
<p>The toy constraint is g(θ) = ½[(θ₁ + 0.25)² + (θ₂ − 0.15)²] − 0.405 ≤ 0. Its Hessian is the identity, so ℓ = 1 is an exact curvature bound; the conservativeness slider scales ℓ up to 3. The toy loss is L(θ) = ½(θ − θ*)ᵀA(θ − θ*), with A having eigenvalues 1 and 8 and the minimum θ* outside the feasible disk. The raw step is Δθ<sub>raw</sub> = −η∇L(θₖ), clipped to a trust radius of 0.5, within which the smoothness bound is used.</p>
<p>The bank at iteration k is D = [Δθₖ₋₁, Δθ<sub>raw</sub>]: the previous accepted update and the current raw step. When the two are nearly collinear the bank drops to the raw step alone. With sampled values Gᵢ = g(θₖ + dᵢ), S = DᵀD, and g₀ = g(θₖ), the green region is the set of updates Dξ inside the trust ball that satisfy Equation (29):</p>
<p class="bound-equation">(1 − 1ᵀξ)g₀ + Gξ + ½ℓ [ξᵀSξ + |ξ|ᵀdiag(S)] ≤ 0.</p>
<p>The projected step minimizes ‖Dξ − Δθ<sub>raw</sub>‖² over this region; coefficients may be negative, so the region is not a convex hull of the candidates. Armijo backtracking then shortens the step if needed. The region is convex and contains the origin, so a shortened step stays certified. The accepted update becomes the next iteration’s first candidate.</p>
<p>Only point evaluations of g and the bound ℓ enter the construction; the true feasible disk is drawn for comparison only. In two dimensions a single disk constraint leaves no certified direction once the iterate reaches the boundary, so the steps shrink to zero there. In a high-dimensional weight space, far more directions can decrease the loss without pushing on an active constraint.</p>
</div></details></div>
</section>

<section id="method" class="section wrap" aria-labelledby="method-title"><div class="section-heading"><div><p class="eyebrow">The method</p><h2 id="method-title">Evaluate. Approximate. <br>Project. Check.</h2></div><p>Each update is chosen from the span of a small bank of candidate updates, so the projection is solved over a few coefficients rather than over every network weight.</p></div>
<div class="algorithm-steps"><article><span>01</span><h3>Propose a gradient step</h3><p>Starting from feasible weights, take a task-loss gradient step and scale it to the local trust region.</p></article><article><span>02</span><h3>Evaluate safety</h3><p>Evaluate the constraints at a bank of candidate weight changes, and combine the samples with local smoothness bounds to form a feasible region.</p></article><article><span>03</span><h3>Solve the projection</h3><p>Solve a small convex QCQP for the feasible combination of candidates closest to the raw step.</p></article><article><span>04</span><h3>Validate and accept</h3><p>Backtrack until the loss decreases and re-check the constraints. If the check fails, tighten the bounds and re-solve.</p></article></div>
<div class="method-details"><div><p class="eyebrow">Work in an update subspace</p><p class="equation">Δθ = Dξ</p><p>The columns of D are sampled candidate updates; SCPO solves for their coefficients ξ. The size of the projection problem is set by the number of candidates and constraints, not by the number of network weights.</p><a class="text-link" href="https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13788v3#S5">See Section 5 and Algorithm 1 ↗</a></div><div><p class="eyebrow">A safe starting point for control</p><h3>A frozen backup.<br>A trainable residual.</h3><p>For control tasks, a zero-initialized residual policy is added to a stabilizing backup controller, so training starts from a controller that is exactly the backup. Near the target, the backup acts alone.</p></div></div>
<div class="guarantee-box"><div><p class="eyebrow">Guarantees</p><h3>Feasibility by induction.<br>Descent by construction.</h3></div><div><p><strong>Feasibility:</strong> starting from a feasible policy, every accepted iterate satisfies the constraints, provided the local smoothness bounds are valid.</p><p><strong>Descent:</strong> a nonzero projected step is a descent direction for the task loss, and Armijo backtracking guarantees decrease under standard smoothness assumptions.</p><p><strong>Control stability:</strong> when the input, invariance, and Lyapunov-margin conditions hold on the target region, the closed loop remains invariant and locally asymptotically stable.</p></div></div>
</section>

<aside class="scope-note"><h3>What is actually checked?</h3><p>The implementation uses a curated finite validation set as a surrogate for exhaustive verification. Empirically estimated curvature bounds and successful checks do not by themselves prove safety on unseen scenarios. Raw proposals and rejected candidates are evaluated before acceptance; they need not be safe to execute on a physical system.</p></aside>

<section class="section wrap intro-grid perspective-section" aria-labelledby="perspective-title"><div><p class="eyebrow">Perspective</p><h2 id="perspective-title">A constrained update. <br>An adaptable policy.</h2></div><div class="prose"><p>SCPO connects projected gradient learning with rollout-based constraint evaluation. Its local geometry comes from candidate updates and safety metrics in weight space.</p><p>The projection can be conservative. It recombines directions already in the sample bank and may return a zero update when that bank offers no feasible descent direction.</p><p>The paper identifies better safe exploration and safety-validation design as directions for future work.</p></div></section>
